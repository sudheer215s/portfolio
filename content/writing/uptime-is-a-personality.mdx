A dropped lookup on a national call-routing system is not a slow page. It is a call that never connects. Uptime, for that service, is a product property.

At C-DOT I rebuilt the PostgreSQL primary-replica cluster behind the IMS/ENUM routing backend: streaming replication, automatic failover, 99.99% uptime, recovery under 30 seconds, and no unplanned outages since that launch. The service does millions of subscriber lookups a day, and the budget on the critical path is sub-second.

## The number next to it

Query-plan analysis and an index redesign made the critical IMS/ENUM queries 40% faster under peak load. Monitoring and alerting moved detection from "a user reported it" to minutes.

The year before, as a research engineer on the same lab's live services, Ubuntu and PostgreSQL held 99.9% availability, and deployment automation cut manual effort by 70%.

None of this is a model story. It is the reason I trust a latency budget, a failover drill, and a metric I can page on more than a demo that worked once.
